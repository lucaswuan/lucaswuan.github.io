01 / CONTEXT

Overview

I competed in VEX robotics on team 2088D. Over the 2022–23 season we built two versions of our robot: a flywheel design that qualified us for the World Championship, and a catapult rebuild. I built the intake, the flywheel, and the catapult, and I programmed the autonomous routines.

02 / CONTRIBUTION

The work

Design 1: the flywheel robot

Our first robot collected discs with an intake and launched them with a spinning flywheel. I built both, then tuned the flywheel speed and the intake spacing to make shots more consistent and reduce jams. This is the robot that placed second in Alberta Provincial Robot Skills and qualified us for Worlds.

Autonomous routines

I programmed the robot’s autonomous routines: the code that drives it on its own at the start of each match and in programming skills runs.

Design 2: the catapult rebuild

Even after tuning, the flywheel wasn’t as reliable as we wanted. After qualifying for Worlds, we rebuilt the robot around a catapult, which launches a whole stack of discs in one motion. I built the catapult.

03 / REFLECTION

Outcome

The flywheel robot placed second in Alberta Provincial Robot Skills and qualified our team for the VEX Robotics World Championship, where we competed in Dallas in 2023. Its reliability problems led to the catapult rebuild.
